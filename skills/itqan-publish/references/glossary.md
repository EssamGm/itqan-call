# Itqan glossary and known transcription errors

The speech model has no idea what these calls are about. It is guessing at
Saudi dialect with no context, so the errors it makes are predictable: real
words that sound close, in place of names and terms that recur in every call.
Everything here exists because it actually appeared in a transcript.

Add to this file whenever a new one shows up. It is the part of the pipeline
that compounds — every correction recorded here is one that never has to be
made again.

## Names and identity

| Correct | Seen as |
|---|---|
| إتقان | اتقان, أتقان, اتكان |
| عصام | (usually right) |
| يونس | يونيس, يونز |
| المتدرب | المدجرب, المتجرب |

The programme is **إتقان** with a hamza — it is the brand, and getting it wrong
in a caption is worse than any other single error, because it is the one word
every viewer will recognise.

## Programme vocabulary

Recurring subject matter, so these are worth knowing:

- **تعلم اللغة الإنجليزية** — the subject of the programme
- **المدرب / المتدرب** — coach / trainee. These two look alike and get swapped.
- **دورة, دورات** — course(s)
- **معهد, معاهد** — institute(s). Heard as "معادل".
- **محتوى** — content
- **Udemy → يوديمي** — appears as a platform name; either spelling is fine, but
  keep it consistent within one video.

## Verified error patterns

Real examples from real calls, with what was actually said:

| Heard | Actually |
|---|---|
| فيه معادل فيه دورات | فيه معاهد فيه دورات |
| فيه كتوب | فيه كتب |
| ويدفعون أموال ومدلوية | ويدفعون أموال وما أدري إيش |
| دصر | بس صح |
| وما بالله ايش | وما أدري إيش |

The pattern is worth understanding rather than memorising: the model
substitutes a common word for an uncommon one that sounds similar. When a line
reads as nonsense, the fix is usually a near-homophone that makes sense in
context.

## What not to "correct"

Saudi dialect is not broken Modern Standard Arabic. Leave it alone:

- **كده, كذا, ايش, وش, ليش, عشان** — all correct as spoken
- **حقتي, حقه** — dialectal possessive, correct
- **بس** — "but/just", correct
- Code-switching mid-sentence into English is normal in these calls and should
  be preserved exactly as spoken

Rewriting dialect into formal Arabic makes the captions read as a translation
of the conversation rather than a record of it, and loses the voice that makes
the content worth publishing in the first place.
